Additional teaching studies

HBB/CRA honeybee tissues: proteins and RNA

Zhong et al. Omics Insights Into the Effects of Highbush Blueberry and Cranberry Crop Agroecosystems on Honey Bee Health and Physiology. Proteomics, online 2025; issue 2026. The author selected this published cohort for BIOC557. Download matched teaching data, R analysis and reproducible preparation code.

The explicit author crosswalk pairs 60 exposed t2 tissue pools across proteomics and RNA. These are 20 apiary-year units, each with head, abdomen and midgut measurements. Analyze one tissue at a time: 10 blueberry and 10 cranberry pools per tissue. The three tissues from one apiary-year are related observations, not three independent apiaries. All other crops, timepoints, unexposed controls and QXB experiments are excluded. Metadata includes collection year, region and MS plate, with exact apiary coordinates omitted.

Protein preparation starts with the author’s positive linear abundance table: mark nonpositive values missing, take log2, and aggregate uniquely mapped RefSeq proteins to genes by median of observed log2 values. RNA starts with the original HTSeq count files, removing auxiliary __ rows. TMM factors are estimated with edgeR from all assigned genes within each tissue; exact gene aliases are aggregated and converted to log2(TMM-normalized CPM+1). Require CPM≥1 in at least half the tissue pools. Retain at most 1,200 variable features per view without crop labels; the default browser/R model then selects 100 per view. No significance-selected, imputed or tissue/year-residualized tables are used. The supplied exact gene mappings identify 161, 163 and 170 genes shared by the head, abdomen and midgut input pools, respectively. A new MOFA teaching reanalysis is provided, not a reproduction of the paper’s WGCNA.

Bee functional interpretation uses 3,382 bee GO BP terms and 149 bee KEGG pathways. Direct UniProt Apis mellifera (taxon 7460) BP annotations were retrieved on 2026-11-11, retaining available BP ancestors/names from GO.db 3.19.1; electronic annotations are included, and newer terms missing from that older ontology snapshot are excluded. KEGG memberships use exact ame gene-to-pathway links, excluding broad overview maps. No human ortholog assignments are made. Every query reports mapped coverage and its annotated model-gene background. Crop, region and acquisition plate may covary; classroom two-group tests are observational and unadjusted. Exact source hashes, crosswalk and transformations accompany the R kit.

Human pancreatic islets: RNA, protein and metabolite features

Multiomics Analysis Reveals Insights into Potential Drivers of Pancreatic Islet Pathology in Type 2 Diabetes. ACS Omega, 2025. Public source tables. The protein table contains six donors; an exact RRID intersection retains three non-diabetic and three T2D donors across all three assays. Technical metabolomics injections are collapsed by the median of nonzero observations per donor; zero intensity is marked missing. Positive heavy:light protein ratios are log2 transformed. RNA is normalized to counts per million using all source count rows before ambiguous gene-label filtering, then transformed as log2(CPM+1). Untargeted metabolite features retain m/z and retention time and have no confirmed compound IDs.

This six-donor dataset illustrates methods and small-sample limitations; group differences can reflect age, sex, islet purity and other covariates. Processed teaching data and exact source-file hashes · Data + R scripts.

Honeybee eggs: lipidomics and metabolomics

Chapman et al. Honey bee egg composition changes seasonally and after acute maternal virus infection. Scientific Reports, 2025. The public supplementary LC–MS tables and queen metadata yield 81 exactly matched queen identities (18 May, 63 June). Lipid-positive measurements form one view; positive and negative metabolite modes are combined into a second view. The teaching input preserves all 367 source lipids and 997 metabolite signals with their source row IDs and putative labels. Source tables flag all these features as passing CV<30%; positive intensities are log2 transformed, and zeros remain missing.

Queen identity links egg pools across assays. Collection month is an observational seasonal grouping, with collection date, beekeeper and environmental confounding. This dataset measures lipidomics and metabolomics. Features retain their source chemical annotations. Processed teaching data and source hashes · Data + R scripts. Public repository: Metabolomics Workbench ST003729.

The classroom pipeline

  1. Check unique, exact matching cell-line IDs; align by ID.
  2. Filter features by missingness and nonzero variance. At the default 30% threshold,919 protein,1200 RNA and 225 metabolite features remain.
  3. At the recommended settings, protein and RNA use the same100 verified genes in the same order; metabolites use100 chemical features. The CCLE default uses Pareto scaling and 5 factors. Gene selection uses the omics measurements alone.
  4. For principal component analysis (PCA) and distances only, fill remaining gaps with feature medians. Joint PCA divides each view by √selected feature count before concatenation.
  5. Multi-Omics Factor Analysis (MOFA) uses separate Gaussian views with gaps retained:2–6 selectable factors,300 maximum iterations,seed 557,mofapy2 0.7.5. Internally prefix feature IDs by view so the same gene can occur in RNA and proteins.
  6. Welch tests use observed values with at least 3 per group. Skin minus Breast effects and Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate (BH FDR) q values are computed across all retained features within each view.

Gene alignment and reviewed starting settings

Protein and RNA are paired by explicit, unique gene identifiers; the crosswalk records the feature ID in each assay. Ambiguous mappings are excluded. After separate missingness and variance checks, their verified intersection is ranked by the sum of within-view variance percentile ranks. The same top 100 genes, in exactly the same order, enter both model matrices. Each assay keeps its own measured values. Metabolites keep chemical feature IDs. QC and differential tables retain all eligible source features.

Default MOFA settings were compared at K=2–6 with unit-variance or Pareto feature scaling, using three repeats that mask 10% of observed entries. Means and SDs use only training observations. Error is averaged across views on a standardized scale. For the classroom default, allow at most one factor per four samples (K≤5 for the 20-pool bee tissues). Among admissible candidates within one standard error of the lowest error, select the smallest K, then the lowest error. The comparison evaluates reconstruction within the selected cohort and 100-feature input pool. Defaults balance reconstruction and model size, using the omics measurements alone. Centering is on, whole-view scaling is off, and the iteration limit is 300. Inspect all comparisons and selected defaults.

The six-donor islet demo starts with PCA; MOFA K=2 is available for comparison. Factors are displayed in descending mean reconstruction contribution across molecular views, so the first two axes show the strongest fitted sources of variation. See the MOFA guidance on normalization, sample size and factor count.

Try factor count, centering and scaling

The factor-count comparison fits K2–6 on the same inputs and reports total in-sample reconstruction per view. Reference patterns are matched by absolute Spearman score correlation and one-to-one assignment, allowing signs and order to change. Use the reconstruction plot and factor-matching table to compare the selected models. See the official MOFA FAQ.

Feature scaling divides each feature by its SD (unit variance), by √SD (Pareto), or leaves its scale unchanged. Centering subtracts feature means across the whole cohort; it does not center the Breast and Skin lineages separately. Whole-view scaling divides a complete molecular matrix by one global SD after optional centering. With center off, reconstruction uses an uncentered zero reference and includes mean levels. Its denominator differs from centered variation.

Reconstruction uses the actual post-centering/post-view-scaling input and its original missing-value mask. Total R² is computed from the combined factor predictions; individual factor contributions can overlap.

Feature networks

Each view contributes its own top-weight features to the network. Factor-link widths are scaled within view so weaker layers remain visible; actual weights and view contributions remain visible. The alternative co-variation mode computes observed, pairwise-complete Spearman correlations across molecular views, requires at least 10 paired observations, applies the chosen absolute-correlation threshold, and shows at most100 edges. The two modes display fitted factor weights and observed molecular co-variation.

GO, Reactome and metabolite pathways

The offline gene catalogs contain 10,217 GO biological-process terms from an explicit 2024 org.Hs.eg.db/GO.db snapshot and 2,116 measured-overlap Reactome terms from release 98. Metabolite memberships use153 KEGG reference maps with human counterparts. Gene catalogs cover the bundled human teaching gene pools. Catalog provenance is retained in the annotation file.

Metabolites:84 unique name matches plus 3 independently checked aliases;66 of these features have usable pathway memberships. 113 ambiguous entries (including lipid sum compositions and isomer mixtures) and 25 unmatched features are excluded from compound-level pathway assignments. Inspect every mapping decision.

One-sided hypergeometric over-representation analysis (ORA) uses the current model-view's features that have catalog membership as background, since those are eligible for weight ranking. Minimum background term size is 3 genes or2 metabolites. BH correction includes every tested term, even zero-query overlaps, separately for each factor/view/catalog query. Results report overlaps, background size and BH q values for each selected factor/view query. The chart states how many terms meet q<0.05.

The metabolite coverage view counts mapped compounds among all retained measured features. Coverage is a count of mapped measured compounds. GO is a gene ontology; compound identities are used for metabolite pathway membership.

Functional Summary and PDF

The final Summary chapter uses the current completed model. Positive- and negative-weight lists are annotated separately with Gene Ontology, Reactome or species-matched KEGG catalogs. Views contributing at least 1% reconstruction enter the annotation queries. Terms with at least two query hits are grouped by common model genes, using complete-linkage clustering at Cohen kappa ≥0.5 and at least three shared model genes. This local grouping follows the shared-gene principle of DAVID functional annotation clustering; it uses ordinary hypergeometric probabilities and complete linkage rather than the DAVID EASE score and fuzzy algorithm.

Benjamini-Hochberg correction is reported within each query and across all term tests in the Summary. A BH-supported group contains a term with report-wide q<0.05. The cluster score is the mean of −log10(raw term p), a ranking summary rather than a new cluster-level p value. Functional labels follow the representative annotation. Each factor has at most one interpretation, requiring report-wide q<0.05, at least three supporting genes, at least 10% reconstruction in its supporting view, centered inputs and at least 16 samples. Literature support is reviewed for the specific annotation and tissue; each retained interpretation links two primary papers and states what they support. Other factors show functional results without a proposed mechanism. Literature metadata was checked against PubMed and CrossRef. The report exports its current parameters, themes, selected features, interpretations, references and abbreviation glossary as PDF or JSON.

Abbreviations: full names and explanations

Pareto: Pareto scaling. Divides each feature by the square root of its standard deviation; centering is controlled separately.

ρ: Spearman rank correlation coefficient. Describes how two quantities vary together by rank.

κ: Cohen kappa similarity. Measures agreement between term-gene membership profiles beyond chance.

n: Observed sample count. The number of paired observations used for a result.

UniProt: Universal Protein Resource. A curated resource of protein sequences and functional annotations.

RNA: Ribonucleic acid. Carries and regulates genetic messages; RNA abundance is one molecular layer.

DNA: Deoxyribonucleic acid. Stores the genetic sequence.

MOFA: Multi-Omics Factor Analysis. Finds latent factors that describe variation across several molecular layers.

MOFA2: Multi-Omics Factor Analysis, version 2. The R software used to fit and inspect MOFA models.

PCA: Principal Component Analysis. Summarizes the main directions of variation in a matrix.

PC: Principal Component. One axis obtained from principal component analysis.

GO: Gene Ontology. A structured vocabulary describing gene functions and biological processes.

BP: Biological Process. The Gene Ontology branch describing biological activities.

GO_BP: Gene Ontology: Biological Process. The biological-process annotation catalog used here.

KEGG: Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes. A database linking genes and compounds to pathways.

ORA: Over-Representation Analysis. Tests whether an annotation occurs more often in a selected feature list than in its background.

DAVID: Database for Annotation, Visualization and Integrated Discovery. A resource for gene annotation, enrichment and grouping related functional terms.

EASE: Expression Analysis Systematic Explorer. The modified Fisher-test score used by DAVID.

BH: Benjamini-Hochberg procedure. Adjusts p values for multiple comparisons.

FDR: False Discovery Rate. The expected proportion of false discoveries among selected results under the testing procedure.

q: BH-adjusted p value in this lab. A p value adjusted with the Benjamini-Hochberg procedure.

p: p value. The probability of a result at least this extreme under the specified null model.

R²: Coefficient of determination. Fraction of the model input reconstructed relative to a zero baseline; with centering on, it describes variation around the mean.

K: Number of latent factors. The chosen number of factors in the model.

SD: Standard Deviation. Measures the spread of a feature. Unit-variance scaling divides by SD; Pareto scaling divides by its square root.

QC: Quality Control. Checks measurement gaps, scales and sample patterns.

CPM: Counts Per Million. RNA counts divided by the effective library size, multiplied by one million.

TMM: Trimmed Mean of M-values. An RNA library-size normalization method that trims extreme log-ratios.

TPM: Transcripts Per Million. RNA abundance adjusted for transcript length and library composition.

TMT: Tandem Mass Tag. An isotope-labeling method for multiplexed protein quantification.

MS: Mass Spectrometry. Measures ions by mass-to-charge ratio.

LC: Liquid Chromatography. Separates compounds before measurement.

LC–MS: Liquid Chromatography-Mass Spectrometry. Combines compound separation with mass-spectrometric detection.

MS/MS: Tandem Mass Spectrometry. Fragments ions to help identify molecules.

m/z: Mass-to-charge ratio. The mass of an ion divided by its charge.

RT: Retention Time. The time at which a compound elutes from a chromatography column.

HBB: Highbush Blueberry. One crop environment in the honeybee study.

CRA: Cranberry. The other crop environment in the honeybee study.

CCLE: Cancer Cell Line Encyclopedia. A collection of molecular measurements from cancer cell-line models.

T2D: Type 2 Diabetes. The disease group in the human-islet dataset.

RRID: Research Resource Identifier. The source identifier used to match the islet donors.

ID: Identifier. A stable label used to match samples or molecular features.

LOC: Locus identifier prefix. An NCBI label for a gene locus; its number is an identifier rather than a functional name.

GeneID: NCBI Gene Identifier. A database identifier for a gene.

NCBI: National Center for Biotechnology Information. Maintains Gene, PubMed and other biological databases.

WGCNA: Weighted Gene Co-expression Network Analysis. Groups genes with correlated expression patterns.

HTSeq: High-Throughput Sequencing analysis framework. Software used to count RNA-sequencing reads.

RNA-seq: RNA Sequencing. Measures RNA by sequencing RNA-derived molecules.

ATP: Adenosine Triphosphate. A molecule used for cellular energy transfer.

ROS: Reactive Oxygen Species. Reactive oxygen-containing molecules involved in signaling and oxidative stress.

TCA: Tricarboxylic Acid cycle. A central metabolic pathway that oxidizes carbon fuels.

Dscam: Down syndrome cell adhesion molecule. A cell-adhesion gene with roles in neuronal wiring and extensive alternative splicing.

DOI: Digital Object Identifier. A persistent identifier for a publication.

PMID: PubMed Identifier. The identifier of a PubMed record.

SVG: Scalable Vector Graphics. An image format that preserves editable text and vector shapes.

PNG: Portable Network Graphics. A raster image format.

PDF: Portable Document Format. A document format suited to saving and printing the report.

CSV: Comma-Separated Values. A plain-text table format.

JSON: JavaScript Object Notation. A structured format used to save data and analysis results.

R: R programming language. The language used in the downloadable analysis scripts.

RDS: R serialized data. An R file containing a saved object.

HDF5: Hierarchical Data Format, version 5. A format used to store the fitted MOFA model.

NA: Not Available. A missing value.

UBC: University of British Columbia. The institution offering BIOC557.

BIOC557: Biochemistry course 557. The course associated with this lab.
